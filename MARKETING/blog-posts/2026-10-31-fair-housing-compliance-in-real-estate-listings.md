## What the Fair Housing Act Actually Prohibits in Listings

Let's start with the basics. The Fair Housing Act makes it illegal to discriminate in housing based on seven protected classes:

- Race or color
- National origin
- Religion
- Sex (including gender identity and sexual orientation)
- Familial status (presence of children, pregnancy)
- Disability
- (Some states add more: source of income, age, marital status, military status)

In real estate listings, discrimination happens two ways:

**Explicit discrimination:** "No children allowed" or "Adults only community." These are obvious violations.

**Steering or code words:** Using phrases that imply a preference for or against a protected group. "Great for young professionals" suggests you don't want families. "Master bedroom" has racial connotations some experts now flag. "Walk to St. Mary's Church" implies a religious preference.

Here's the tricky part: you're allowed to describe the property. You're not allowed to describe the *ideal resident*.

**Bad:** "Perfect for a growing family with two kids and a dog."
**Good:** "Three bedrooms, fenced backyard, close to parks."

**Bad:** "Retirement community. Must be 55+."
**Good:** "Active adult community verified as 55+ housing per HUD guidelines."

**Bad:** "Beautiful home in the Italian neighborhood."
**Good:** "Beautiful home near Little Italy's restaurants and shops."

Notice the difference? The good versions describe features, not people. They talk about what the *property* offers, not who should live there.

## 7 Phrases That Sound Innocent but Violate Fair Housing Rules

Even experienced agents slip up with these. Here are the most common violations hiding in plain sight:

### 1. Family-related language
- **Avoid:** "Starter home," "family room," "good for kids," "empty nester special"
- **Why:** These imply preferences about who should live there
- **Use instead:** "Three bedrooms," "open floor plan," "main-floor primary suite"

### 2. Religious references
- **Avoid:** "Near St. John's," "great Catholic school district," "perfect for Jewish families"
- **Why:** You're not allowed to imply a religious preference
- **Use instead:** "Near private schools," "top-rated school district," "walking distance to houses of worship" (if you must, keep it generic)

### 3. Age-related descriptions
- **Avoid:** "Young professional's dream," "retirement paradise," "ideal for college students"
- **Why:** Age discrimination is prohibited (except for verified 55+ communities)
- **Use instead:** "Close to downtown," "single-level living," "near university campus"

### 4. Racial or ethnic descriptors
- **Avoid:** "Desirable neighborhood," "historic ethnic area," "safe area" (this one is loaded)
- **Why:** These often carry coded racial meaning
- **Use instead:** "Well-maintained neighborhood," "tree-lined streets," "low crime rate" (if you have verified data)

### 5. Disability-related language
- **Avoid:** "Not suitable for wheelchair users," "great for able-bodied buyers," "no stairs" (implies a preference against those who need stairs)
- **Why:** You can't imply the property is unsuitable for someone with a disability
- **Use instead:** "Single-level floor plan," "step-free entry," "wide doorways" (if true)

### 6. Gender-specific terms
- **Avoid:** "Master bedroom," "man cave," "his and hers closets"
- **Why:** "Master" has racial history, and gendered terms can imply preferences
- **Use instead:** "Primary suite," "bonus room," "dual walk-in closets"

### 7. Marital or familial status
- **Avoid:** "Great for singles," "perfect for a couple," "ideal for a bachelor"
- **Why:** You can't imply the property is for a specific household type
- **Use instead:** "One-bedroom layout," "cozy space," "efficient floor plan"

**Real example of a violation:**
> *"Charming 3-bedroom home in a quiet, family-friendly neighborhood. Perfect for parents with young children. Walking distance to elementary school and community playground."*

**Compliant version:**
> *"Charming 3-bedroom home on a quiet street. Walking distance to elementary school and community playground. Fenced backyard with play area."*

The second version describes features without dictating who should use them.

## How to Write Compliant Listing Descriptions (With Before/After Examples)

The best test? Read your listing out loud. If you can replace any phrase with "This home is perfect for [protected class]," you probably have a problem.

Here's a framework for writing compliant copy every time:

**The 3-Question Check:**
1. Does this describe the property or the person?
2. Would I say this about any buyer?
3. Does this imply a preference about who lives here?

If you answer "the person" or "no" or "yes," rewrite it.

### Before and After Examples

**Before (violation):**
> *"Stunning home in the heart of the historic district. Original character throughout. Perfect for empty nesters looking to downsize or young professionals who want to be near nightlife. Walk to St. Mary's Church and the community pool."*

**After (compliant):**
> *"Stunning home in the historic district. Original character throughout. 2,100 square feet with main-floor primary suite. Walk to restaurants, parks, and community pool. Low-maintenance yard."*

**Before (violation):**
> *"Great starter home for a young family. Three bedrooms, big backyard for the kids to play. Safe neighborhood with lots of other families. Walking distance to the elementary school."*

**After (compliant):**
> *"Three-bedroom home with fenced backyard. Located on a quiet street near parks and an elementary school. Open kitchen with granite counters. Move-in ready."*

**Before (violation):**
> *"Accessible home for disabled buyers. Wide doorways, roll-in shower. Perfect for wheelchair users."*

**After (compliant):**
> *"Wide doorways throughout. Roll-in shower on main level. Step-free entry from garage. Single-level living."*

Notice the compliant versions don't lose selling power. They just focus on features instead of people.

## How to Handle "55+" and Senior Housing Listings Correctly

Senior housing is the one area where age restrictions are legal—but only under specific conditions.

The Housing for Older Persons Act (HOPA) allows housing to be restricted to people 55+ if:
- At least 80% of units are occupied by at least one person 55+
- The community publishes and follows policies showing intent to operate as 55+
- The community verifies occupancy regularly

**What you can say:**
- "Active adult community"
- "55+ community verified per HOPA guidelines"
- "Age-restricted community, residents must be 55+"

**What you cannot say:**
- "Retirement community" (without verification)
- "No children" (even in a 55+ community, some exceptions apply)
- "Adults only"

**Bad example:**
> *"Quiet retirement community. No children allowed. Perfect for seniors who want peace and quiet."*

**Good example:**
> *"Verified 55+ active adult community per HUD guidelines. Clubhouse, pool, and lawn care included. Two-bedroom condo with main-floor primary suite."*

If you're listing a property in a 55+ community, include the verification language. Don't make assumptions about age restrictions based on reputation alone.

## What Happens When You Violate Fair Housing Rules? (Real Consequences)

This isn't a "maybe they'll catch it" situation. Fair housing enforcement is active, and penalties are severe.

**HUD penalties (per violation):**
- First violation: Up to $21,663
- Second violation within 5 years: Up to $54,157
- Third or more violations: Up to $108,315

**Beyond fines:**
- License suspension or revocation
- Required fair housing training (at your expense)
- Lawsuits from private parties (no cap on damages)
- MLS suspension or expulsion
- Reputation damage that follows you

**Real case:** In 2022, a California agent was fined $25,000 and required to take 40 hours of fair housing training for listing copy that said "perfect for a Christian family." The agent claimed it was an accident. HUD didn't care.

**Another case:** A New York brokerage paid $225,000 to settle claims that agents used "code words" like "desirable neighborhood" and "good schools" to steer buyers based on race.

The pattern is clear: ignorance isn't a defense. Your listing copy is public record. Anyone can file a complaint.

## 5 Practical Steps to Stay Compliant Every Time You Write a Listing

Here's your checklist for every listing you write:

### Step 1: Remove all people descriptions
Scan for words like "perfect for," "ideal for," "great for," "suited for." Replace them with feature descriptions.

**Before:** "Perfect for first-time buyers"
**After:** "Low down payment options available"

### Step 2: Check for religious, ethnic, or cultural references
Remove mentions of specific churches, synagogues, mosques, or cultural centers unless you're describing a nearby landmark generically.

**Allowed:** "Located near downtown with restaurants and shops"
**Not allowed:** "Walking distance to the Greek Orthodox Church"

### Step 3: Verify any "55+" or age-restricted language
If the property isn't in a verified 55+ community, don't mention age at all. If it is, include the verification language.

### Step 4: Use neutral, feature-based language
Describe square footage, bedrooms, bathrooms, lot size, upgrades, location features (parks, transit, schools—not "good" schools, just "nearby" schools).
