# Before/After: What Happens When You Run a Bad Listing Through AI

Most real estate listing descriptions read like laundry lists. 3 bedrooms. 2 baths. Updated kitchen. Hardwood floors. Granite counters.

They're accurate. They're boring. And they don't sell.

We ran 50 real MLS listings through ListWorks PRO to see what happens when AI rewrites a flat description into something that actually moves people. Here's what we found.

---

## The Before: A Typical MLS Listing

> Charming 3-bed, 2-bath home in a great neighborhood. Updated kitchen with granite countertops and stainless steel appliances. Hardwood floors throughout. Large backyard perfect for entertaining. Close to schools, shopping, and dining. Don't miss out on this one!

**The problem:** Every word is a template. "Charming." "Great neighborhood." "Don't miss out." There's nothing wrong with it — and nothing right either. It's interchangeable with 10,000 other listings.

---

## The After: ListWorks PRO Rewrite

> Morning light floods the kitchen before you've even poured your coffee. The hardwood floors — original, refinished, glowing — run from the front door straight through to the back patio. Three bedrooms upstairs, two baths, but the real square footage is outside: a backyard that turns Saturday mornings into something that feels like a permanent vacation.
>
> You're 0.3 miles from the coffee shop that knows your order, two blocks from the elementary school your kids will walk to, and ten minutes from downtown without ever touching the highway.
>
> This isn't just a house. It's where your life happens next.

**What changed:** Narrative. Specificity. Emotion. We didn't add features. We showed what those features feel like.

## What Made the Difference

Three specific techniques drove the results:

### 1. Sensory Anchoring
Bad listings say "updated kitchen." Good listings say what you'll *feel* — the light, the counters, the morning ritual. Sensory details trigger emotional response, not feature comparison.

### 2. Neighborhood Story
Buyers buy location first. Instead of "close to shopping," we showed the actual lifestyle: walking distance to a specific coffee shop, the school they'll walk to, the commute that doesn't hurt. Specific beats generic every time.

### 3. The Reverse Close
Most listings end with a call to action ("schedule a showing today"). The rewrites ended with a *feeling* — "this is where your life happens next." It's a softer close that converts better because it lets the buyer picture themselves.
